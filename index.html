What the creators state

The official page frames the experience as becoming an eligible single rat, meeting 21 contestants, holding intimate conversations, and eliminating candidates round by round. Mustakos describes a roughly ten-minute structure in which players select seven contestants and give roses.

01

02

03

Reaction and rejection

Brief replies trigger expressive reactions; roses later transform light conversation into consequence.

Player-action events observed

  1. Choose one of four Ratchelor portraits.
  2. Cast seven contestants from a grid of 21.
  3. Advance into a one-on-one conversation.
  4. Respond with a short reaction such as “Yuck,” “Wow!” or “Yikes.”
  5. Read the character’s animated emotional response.
  6. Give roses to decide who continues into later rounds.
My interpretation — not a creator claim

The game’s strength is not branching complexity; it is the clear rhythm of notice → choose → react → commit. AFTERIMAGE borrows that readable rhythm while replacing romance selection with decisions about what is real.

Research, gaps, decision

Verified

Algorat identifies itself as a four-person rat-centric computational arts collective. The trilogy has more than one million reported playthroughs. Mustakos states that she led art across the series and worked on characters, dialogue, reactions, and interface.

What I could not verify

Selected exchange

AI: “Random glitches could represent hallucination.”

Designer: “Randomness alone makes it decorative. What player action causes the world to change?”

AI: “Let the system remember what the player confirms, then reuse those details as facts.”

Conclusion + design decision

Hallucination becomes a feedback loop between expectation and evidence. The game will use deterministic choice propagation—not random visual noise—so every later distortion can be traced to something the player accepted.

03

Narrative + concept

Hallucination as authored memory

Interpretation

Not seeing “nothing”

Hallucination is the mind confidently completing incomplete evidence. The player does not simply witness distortions; their decisions teach the archive what to show next.

World

A museum after closing

Records, rooms, and labels contradict each other. Accepted details migrate between exhibits until the building forms one unstable history.

Player role

Night archivist

Inspect fragments, answer certainty prompts, and file one official version before sunrise.

Intended experience

Curiosity → doubt → ownership

A quiet, tense fifteen-minute experience in which the final reveal feels surprising but personally caused.

05

Style + sound guide

Warm evidence, uneasy motion

Visual system

Near-black grounds the archive; lilac suggests unstable memory; coral marks evidence; acid green marks player-confirmed “truth.”

Interface

Large framed scenes, short response choices, and immediate reactions are informed by Algorat’s readable conversation loop—not copied imagery.

Reference: Algorat, The Ratchelor ↗

Motion

Objects drift only after a decision. Slow parallax signals doubt; one sharp snap confirms a filed memory. Reduced-motion users receive fades instead.

Original motion direction for AFTERIMAGE.

Sound

Paper, fluorescent hum, distant HVAC, and a dry confirmation click. Each accepted detail adds one pitched layer, making the ending audibly player-authored.

Planned sources: original field recordings and generated Web Audio tones. No third-party track is selected.

Case-study sound note

The Ratchelor’s in-game credits disclose YouTube Audio Library, Freesound, and Mixkit. I could not verify specific files or a named sound designer, so those details are intentionally not attributed.

06

Two-week build plan

Build small, test the loop

Week 2

Build the playable spine

  • Responsive browser shell and one complete room
  • Inspect, compare, and confirm interactions
  • Three-stage choice-state system
  • Five key illustrations and UI motion
  • Generated tone and original ambience prototype
  • Complete ending assembled from stored choices
Deliverable: one 10–15 minute playable path.
Week 3

Test, clarify, refine

  • Test with 5 players on laptop and phone
  • Track where cause-and-effect becomes unclear
  • Rewrite prompts and improve choice feedback
  • Adjust pacing, contrast, sound, and motion
  • Add reduced motion and keyboard support
  • Fix errors and publish the approved build
Success: 4 of 5 testers can explain how they shaped the ending.

Protected scope: one room, three stages, one ending system. Extra rooms, procedural art, and save accounts remain stretch goals.
